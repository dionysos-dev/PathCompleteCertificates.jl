Lift reference

A lift is the lift of an operation: a map from path-complete graphs to path-complete graphs whose nodes stand for combinations of the original nodes ((Debauche et al., 2022), Defs. 5 and 7). It exists to compare graphs: when the lifted G simulates H, H is no worse than G for every template closed under the operation. compare runs every lift on a pair of graphs; see Graph comparison via lifts for the picture. A new lift is one file in src/lifts/catalogue/ answering lift and requirements.

The interface

PathCompleteCertificates.AbstractLift — Type
AbstractLift

A map from path-complete graphs to path-complete graphs whose nodes stand for combinations of the original nodes under one Operation: the lift of an operation (Debauche, Della Rossa & Jungers, Defs. 5 and 7; Debauche, Def. 8.15). It exists to compare graphs. When lift(L, G) simulates H, G ≤_V H holds for every template closed under what the lift requirements: a certificate on G, combined along the simulation, is one on H. compare runs every lift on a pair of graphs.

A lift is an instance. It implements lift and requirements; it inherits simulation, which builds the lifted graph and searches a map into it, and overrides it where something cheaper decides the same question. is_valid is written once.

PathCompleteCertificates.is_valid — Method
is_valid(L, template, system) -> Bool

Whether L is guaranteed not to make the bound worse for template on system — Definition 6 of Debauche, Della Rossa & Jungers: whether $G ≤_V L(G)$ for every path-complete $G$.

Written once: the template is closed under every operation the lift requires (requirements, is_closed_under), or — when the template has_dual — the dual template is closed under what the dual lift requires, on the transposed system. The two questions are the same one (Debauche, Prop. 7.5), so a closure declared on either of a dual pair of templates serves both. A few methods override it where the literature proves validity without closure, and say which theorem.

false never means unsound. Every lift preserves path-completeness, so a certificate on the lifted graph certifies the system regardless; it only carries no guarantee of improvement.

source
PathCompleteCertificates.simulation — Method
simulation(L, G, H; optimizer = nothing)

The simulation of H by lift(L, G), or nothing when there is none. It proves G ≤_V H for every template closed under requirements(L) (Philippe & Jungers, Thm. 3.5; Debauche, Thms. 7.18, 7.55, 7.56, 7.65 and 7.66), and it is the recipe for a certificate on H from one on G.

The default builds the lifted graph and returns the simulation map from the nodes of H, read through origins: subsets, multisets or (node, word) pairs of G. IdentityLift returns the plain map, MinLift the greatest simulation relation, MaxLift that relation on the dual graphs and SumLift() the integer matrix of a linear program, each without building anything. optimizer is read by the linear program only.

PathCompleteCertificates.Operation — Type
Operation

An operation on node functions that a template may be closed under, and that a lift may require (Debauche, Della Rossa & Jungers, Def. 7).

The inhabitants are Addition, Maximum, Minimum, Composition and InverseComposition. A template answers is_closed_under for each; a lift lists its requirements; is_valid is written once against the two.

source
PathCompleteCertificates.dual — Function
dual(x)

The dual of a graph, a lift, an operation, a template, a system, a problem or a certificate; dual(template, V) is the dual of one node function.

Duality is an involution on the whole problem: a certificate on graph for template and system is one on dual(graph) for dual(template) and dual(system) (Debauche, Lemma 6.25), and dual(certificate) constructs it. On an operation it is what the dual lift requires: minimum and maximum exchange (Lemma 7.45), addition is fixed because the sum lift is self-dual (Prop. 7.13), and composition becomes composition with the inverse (Prop. 7.64).

The dual of a lift

PathCompleteCertificates.DualLift — Type
DualLift(L)

The dual of a lift, dual(L)(G) = dual(L(dual(G))) (Debauche, Def. 7.4); dual of the result is L again. Its requirements are the duals of L's, so validity follows the operation table of dual (Prop. 7.5), and its simulation is L's on the dual graphs, since a map preserving labelled edges still does once both graphs are reversed. MaxLift and BackwardCompositionLift are the two here.

The catalogue

PathCompleteCertificates.IdentityLift — Type
IdentityLift()

The lift that returns the graph. Its simulation is the plain simulation map, which decides the order for every template and every system (Philippe & Jungers, Thm. 3.5): when G simulates H, a certificate on G read through the map is one on H in the same template. Requires nothing, and is the first lift compare tries.

source
PathCompleteCertificates.MinLift — Type
MinLift()

Nodes are the nonempty subsets of the original nodes; (A, B, i) is an edge when every node of A has an i-edge into B (Debauche, Della Rossa & Jungers, Def. 9). The node function is the minimum over the subset.

Valid for templates closed under Minimum, and for the linear copositive norms on positive systems without closure (Debauche, Thm. 7.43). Its simulation is the greatest simulation relation between the nodes, a map into subsets computed in polynomial time, and it is complete: G ≤_V H for every min-closed template exactly when the relation exists (Debauche, Thm. 8.1). Built explicitly only for small graphs; origins are the subsets.

source
PathCompleteCertificates.MaxLift — Type
MaxLift()

Nodes are the nonempty subsets; (A, B, i) is an edge when every node of B is reached by an i-edge from A (Debauche, Della Rossa & Jungers, Def. 9). The dual of MinLift (Debauche, Lemma 7.45), valid for templates closed under Maximum; its simulation is the relation on the dual graphs, complete for them (Debauche, Thm. 8.5).

source
PathCompleteCertificates.SumLift — Type
SumLift(T)
SumLift()

The T-sum lift: nodes are the multisets of T original nodes, (P, Q, i) is an edge when the i-edges from P to Q admit a perfect matching (Debauche, Della Rossa & Jungers, Def. 8; Debauche, Prop. 7.16), and the node function is the sum over the multiset. Without T it is the sum lift over every size (Debauche, Della Rossa & Jungers, HSCC 2023, Def. 8), which cannot be built but whose simulation is decided by a linear program.

Valid for templates closed under Addition; self-dual (Debauche, Prop. 7.13). simulation(SumLift(), G, H; optimizer) returns a nonnegative integer matrix C, every row summing to at least one, such that C·V is a certificate on H whenever V is one on G (Philippe, Athanasopoulos, Angeli & Jungers, Def. IV.2 and Thm. IV.4). It is complete: feasible exactly when some T-sum lift of G simulates H (Debauche, Thm. 7.35 and Cor. 8.11). The solver's matrix is rounded to integers and checked against the program again before it is returned. simulation(SumLift(T), G, H) builds the T-th level and returns a map into multisets, which exists for some T whenever the program is feasible (HSCC 2023, Lemma 3). origins are the multisets as sorted vectors.

source
PathCompleteCertificates.CompositionLift — Type
CompositionLift(T)

The T-forward composition lift: a node (s, j₁, …, j_T) for every node s and every T-tuple of modes, and for every edge (a, b, i) and tuple the edge ((a, j₁…j_T), (b, i, j₁…j_{T−1}), j_T) (Debauche, Della Rossa & Jungers, Def. 10; Debauche, Def. 7.58). The node function is $V_s ∘ A_{j_1} ∘ ⋯ ∘ A_{j_T}$.

Valid for templates closed under Composition with the dynamics, which for the templates here means invertible mode matrices. Its simulation is sufficient for the order on such templates (Debauche, Thm. 7.65) but not necessary (Jongeneel & Jungers, Ex. IV.3). Unlike the subset lifts it can strictly improve a bound (Jongeneel & Jungers, Ex. IV.4), which is why Hierarchy accepts it as a step of refine. origins are the (s, [j₁, …, j_T]) pairs.

source
PathCompleteCertificates.BackwardCompositionLift — Type
BackwardCompositionLift(T)

The T-backward composition lift (Debauche, Def. 7.61), the dual of CompositionLift(T) (Prop. 7.64): the node function is $V_s ∘ A_{j_T}^{-1} ∘ ⋯ ∘ A_{j_1}^{-1}$. Valid for templates closed under InverseComposition on invertible systems (Prop. 7.63); its simulation is sufficient for the order on them (Thm. 7.66).

source

Comparing two graphs

PathCompleteCertificates.Comparison — Type
Comparison

What compare returns for two graphs G and H: the lifts tried, in order; the simulations found, one per lift whose lifted G simulates H; and the templates proved no worse on H than on G, among those given.

Read without templates, it is the literature's sentence: H is no worse than G for every template closed under the requirements of each lift that found a simulation, and for every template at all when the identity lift did.

source
PathCompleteCertificates.compare — Method
compare(G, H; templates = (), system = nothing, optimizer = nothing, lifts = …)

Decide whether H is no worse a graph than G — $G ≤_V H$ in Debauche, Della Rossa & Jungers, Def. 4: every system certified on G in a template V is certified on H in the same template — and for which templates.

Each lift in lifts is asked for its simulation of H by the lifted G, cheapest and strongest first: the identity lift, whose map settles the question for every template and stops the search; the min and max lifts, by a relation; the sum lift, by a linear program, tried only when an optimizer is given; the two composition lifts of length one. A template of templates is proved when some lift with a simulation is_valid for it on system, which is required with them. Returns a Comparison. One mode per edge.

source
PathCompleteCertificates.lifts — Method
lifts(comparison)

The lifts compare tried, in the order it tried them. The sum lift over every size is tried only with an optimizer, and nothing after the identity lift once it has found a map.

source
PathCompleteCertificates.simulations — Method
simulations(comparison)

The pairs lift => simulation of a Comparison, one per lift whose lifted graph simulates the second: a map, a relation, a map into multisets or words, or the integer matrix of the sum lift — what simulation returned, and the recipe for a certificate on the second graph from one on the first.

source
PathCompleteCertificates.templates — Method
templates(comparison)

The templates given to compare for which the second graph is proved no worse than the first: those for which some lift with a simulation is_valid on the system.
