Should you use PathCompleteCertificates.jl?

PathCompleteCertificates.jl computes certificates for discrete-time switched linear systems under arbitrary switching: proofs that hold for every sequence of modes. This page helps you decide whether it fits your problem.

When to use it

Use it when two conditions hold:

  1. your system is a discrete-time switched linear system, $x_{k+1} = A_{\sigma(k)} x_k$ or $x_{k+1} = A_{\sigma(k)} x_k + B_{\sigma(k)} u_k$, and any sequence of modes may occur;
  2. you want a proof rather than simulations: that the system is stable, that it stays safe, or that a feedback policy keeps its cost bounded.

It fits best when, in addition:

  • you want to choose the graph of the certificate, or let the package improve it;
  • you want node functions beyond quadratic forms: sums of squares, copositive functions for positive systems, polyhedral norms, or the piecewise quadratic and piecewise linear functions of SetProg.jl;
  • you want to choose the solver: any JuMP solver for the kind of program the template sets, semidefinite or linear, with a log-determinant for optimal control;
  • you study the path-complete framework itself, and want to compare graphs, use duality, benchmark methods from different papers, or add a template, a problem or an algorithm.

Not every combination of problem, template and method is supported yet: What works with what lists them.

When not to use it

This package is not the right tool when:

  • The switching is constrained, for instance by an automaton. Support is planned, see issue #45. Until then, SwitchOnSafety.jl bounds the joint spectral radius under constrained switching, and the MATLAB CSSystem toolbox analyses the stability of systems with constrained switching sequences.
  • You need lower bounds from products of matrices, or the exact joint spectral radius. No method computes the joint spectral radius exactly for every set of matrices. This package gives upper bounds, and lower bounds only from the cycles of a certificate, which sometimes prove the upper bound exact. The MATLAB JSR Toolbox and SwitchOnSafety.jl give lower bounds from products, with Gripenberg's algorithm among others, and the function ipa of the MATLAB ttoolboxes returns the exact value when it finds an invariant polytope.
  • You need controlled invariant sets. SwitchOnSafety.jl computes them for hybrid systems.
  • You need reachable sets rather than certificates. See ReachabilityAnalysis.jl.
  • Your system evolves in continuous time, is nonlinear, or is a hybrid system other than a switched linear one, for instance with switching that depends on the state, or with resets. This package does not cover these systems.
