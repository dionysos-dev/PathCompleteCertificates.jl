Graph improvement via refinements

Every other part of this package takes a graph you chose and solves on it. refine does the opposite: it starts from a graph you do not care about and grows the one the system needs, guided by where the certificate is under strain ((Ninite and Jungers, 2026)). Refinement: letting the graph design itself runs it.

A refinement is a transformation of a path-complete graph that is no worse for every template: a copy inherits its origin's function, a product chains two edge inequalities. The literature calls these lifts too; here the word lift is kept for the lifts that compare graphs, and the transformations that improve one are refinements.

The four atoms

Every refining construction in the literature is one of two local operations at an edge $(s, d, w)$, forward or backward ((Athanasopoulos and Jungers, 2019), Defs. 4 to 7).

RefinementAt one edge (s, d, w)Paper's name
ForwardEdgeSplita copy of s owning that outgoing edge and every incoming onepartial P*-lift, Def. 7
BackwardEdgeSplita copy of d owning that incoming edge and every outgoing onepartial P-lift, Def. 6
ForwardEdgeProductreplace it by (s, d′, w·u) for every (d, d′, u)partial T-lift, Def. 4
BackwardEdgeProductreplace it by (s′, d, u·w) for every (s′, s, u)partial T*-lift, Def. 5

A split adds a node and splits the inequalities that passed through it. A product adds no node and writes a word on an edge: one inequality on $A_x A_w$ with nothing imposed on the state in between, which is sound for stability only. A WordGraph carries words, and its expanded_form reads the same language with one mode per edge; for a template closed under composition the two certify the same rate, the word graph with fewer variables ((Debauche, 2024), Prop. 7.70).

Each backward atom is the dual of the forward one, and the classical hierarchies are the atoms at every edge:

  • ForwardEdgeSplit on outgoing_edges(graph, s), the default step of the loop, gives s one copy per outgoing edge: the forward lift at a node of (Ninite and Jungers, 2026), Def. 3, whenever no two edges of s share a destination, which gives one copy per successor;
  • BackwardEdgeSplit on edges(graph) is the path-dependent lift of (Philippe et al., 2016), Def. 3, PathDependentRefinement(1); iterated from the one-node graph it is de_bruijn;
  • either product on edges(graph) is ProductRefinement(2), the product lift of (Philippe et al., 2016), Def. 2.

None of this needs anything from the template: give every copy the function of the node it came from and the old certificate is still feasible ((Ninite and Jungers, 2026), Thm. 3); two chained edge inequalities imply the product's ((Athanasopoulos and Jungers, 2019), Thm. 1). The one step that is not an atom is a lift, CompositionLift, which can strictly improve a graph for templates closed under composition ((Jongeneel and Jungers, 2025), Ex. IV.4) and which Hierarchy accepts where it is valid.

The loop

A path-complete graph with a quadratic function at each node gives an upper bound $γ^*(G)$ on the joint spectral radius, the optimum of $\min γ$ subject to $γ^2 P_a \succeq A_i^\top P_b A_i$ on every edge $(a, b, i)$ and $P_a \succ 0$; jsr_bound bisects on $γ$. More nodes give a tighter bound and cost more, so the question is where the next node should go. de_bruijn answers by brute force, remembering the last k modes at mᵏ nodes. Refinement answers by looking at the solution. At each step:

  1. Solve. jsr_bound returns a certificate: the rate and one function per node.
  2. Read the strain. An edge is tight when its inequality is active. edge_slacks measures them, tight_edges names them, tight_subgraph keeps only them ((Ninite and Jungers, 2026), Def. 4).
  3. Propose. A strategy reads the certificate and proposes refined graphs.
  4. Refine. With select = :first the first proposal is taken; with select = :best every proposal is solved and the lowest rate kept.
  5. Repeat on the bigger graph.

A refinement never makes the bound worse, so the sequence of rates is monotone.

Strategies

A strategy is any callable (certificate; atol) -> Vector{Refined}. The package ships the three rules of the literature.

StrategyProposesSource
SplitTightNode, the defaultone candidate: ForwardEdgeSplit on the out-star of the node held at the most tight outgoing edges; ties go to the cheapest split, then the lowest node(Ninite and Jungers, 2026), Def. 3
RefineTightEdgesevery atom at every tight edge; with select = :best the one with the smallest rate is kept(Athanasopoulos and Jungers, 2019), Example 2
Hierarchy(step)one candidate: a global refinement, a local one at every edge, or a lift(Philippe et al., 2016); (Jongeneel and Jungers, 2025), §IV-A

Hierarchy(PathDependentRefinement(1)) from the one-node graph walks the De Bruijn hierarchy one order per step, which makes de_bruijn a special case of the loop and a baseline inside it.

Where it starts

refine(template, problem) starts from common_graph of the system: the common Lyapunov function graph, or the automaton under constrained switching, so the loop reads the right language from the first step. refine(template, graph, problem) starts from a graph of yours. Path-completeness is checked on that first graph only; every refinement preserves it.

When it stops

status on the returned RefinementTrace says which of five things happened.

OPTIMALthe rate is the joint spectral radius
STALLEDstall_max steps in a row bought nothing
DEPTH_REACHEDdepth_max reached with the bound still falling
STOPPEDthe stop predicate held, c -> c.rate < 1 being the usual one
EXHAUSTEDthe strategy proposed nothing, and the gap had not closed

Only the first is a certified answer. The trace keeps every certificate and every Refined step, so a run can be read back: rates, graphs, steps and the origins of each node.

Two certificates of optimality

The rate is an upper bound. To know it is the answer you must meet it from below, and there are two independent ways, both checked at every step.

Structural. If every node has at most one outgoing edge in the tight subgraph, then $γ^*(G) = ρ(\mathcal{A})$ ((Ninite and Jungers, 2026), Thm. 4). That is is_jsr_exact: a combinatorial property of which constraints are active proves the bound exact. It is sufficient, not necessary, so false means unknown; and it depends on reading tightness correctly.

Numerical. A cycle $(a_1,a_2,i_1) \dots (a_k,a_1,i_k)$ of the tight subgraph forces $γ \ge ρ(A_{i_k} \cdots A_{i_1})^{1/k}$, and any product of modes bounds the joint spectral radius from below. jsr_lower_bound takes the best cycle, and rate minus bound is a certified interval that does not depend on the tightness tolerance at all, since a cycle of the tight subgraph is still a cycle of the graph.

The two catch different cases. Two opposite rotations scaled by 0.9 have $P = I$ exact on any graph, so every edge is tight, Theorem 4 can never fire, and the loop would split forever at a constant bound; the bracket closes at the first step. Whenever the graph is already right, only the bracket sees it.

The tolerances

rtolbisection tolerance of each jsr_bound call
atolhow close to zero counts as tight
gap_tolhow close the bracket must come to count as closed

atol is the delicate one. Bisection stops just above the optimum, so an active edge does not measure zero but about the bisection gap. Set atol too low and the tight subgraph comes out empty, every node satisfies Theorem 4 vacuously, and the run certifies a bound that is not the joint spectral radius. refine therefore enforces atol ≥ 10 rtol.

Generic in the problem

The loop names no problem. It reads best_certificate to solve a step, objective to compare two steps, edge_slacks to see where a certificate is held and optimality_gap to stop; a problem that answers them joins the loop with the strategies and the trace unchanged. Stability and safety do. Safety has no lower bound, so its runs end on a stall or the depth budget rather than OPTIMAL, with the margin read as c.margin on each certificate of the trace. What works with what says which templates the loop runs on.

PathCompleteCertificates.refine — Function
refine(refinement, graph, edges) -> Refined
refine(refinement, graph) -> Refined

Apply one refinement: at a locus of edges for a local one, to the whole graph for a global one. A local refinement given no locus is applied at every edge. The iterated form, driven by a certificate, is refine(template, graph, problem).
