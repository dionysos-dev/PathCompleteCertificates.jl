Reading a certificate

certify returns a certificate. This page shows what every certificate holds, then what is specific to each problem.

The code on this page uses the three certificates of How a certificate works, on the running example of Systems and problems, named here after what they certify:

template = PCC.QuadraticTemplate()
graph = PCC.common_graph(system)

lyapunov = PCC.certify(template, graph, PCC.StabilityProblem(system);
                       optimizer = Clarabel.Optimizer)
barrier = PCC.certify(template, graph, PCC.SafetyProblem(system, S0, Su);
                      optimizer = Clarabel.Optimizer)
optimizer = JuMP.optimizer_with_attributes(Clarabel.Optimizer,
                                           "chordal_decomposition_enable" => false)
controller = PCC.certify(template, graph, PCC.OptimalControlProblem(controlled, Q, R);
                         optimizer)

Feasible or not

Check is_feasible first:

PCC.is_feasible(lyapunov), PCC.is_feasible(barrier), PCC.is_feasible(controller)
(true, true, true)

An infeasible result means that no certificate was found with this template on this graph, not that the property fails: try another template or another graph. status gives the solver's termination status, for diagnosis.

What every certificate holds

functions(c)the node functions, one per node; for stability, fitted SetProg sets, evaluated with node_value
graph(c)the graph of the certificate
template(c)the template of its node functions
problem(c)the problem it certifies
status(c)the termination status of the solver

Each certificate also holds what its problem certifies: c.rate, c.margin or c.gains, in the sections below.

Calling a certificate

Calling the certificate at a state, c(x), evaluates its common function (see How a certificate works):

Certificate$c(x)$
stabilitythe common Lyapunov function
safetythe common barrier function, negative on the initial set and positive on the unsafe set
optimal controlan upper bound on the worst-case cost of the policy from $x$
x = [1.0, 0.0]
lyapunov(x), barrier(x), controller(x)
(2.586871860959804, -0.5835930570743407, 2.447366870940475)

Tight edges

An edge is tight when its edge inequality is active, with zero slack. edge_slacks measures every edge, and tight_edges lists those whose slack is at most atol, $10^{-4}$ by default:

PCC.edge_slacks(lyapunov), PCC.edge_slacks(barrier), PCC.edge_slacks(controller)
([3.267974360292728e-8, 1.667891589640513e-6], [-1.4537181246103426e-11, -2.116636865778787e-10], [0.00038975229081689156, 0.00037226872418558464])

Both edges are tight for stability and for safety; the inequalities of the controller have slack. Tight edges are what Refining the graph reads to grow the graph.

Tolerances for stability

certify stops within rtol of the smallest rate, so the slacks of a stability certificate are of that order. Read them with an atol at least ten times rtol: the defaults, rtol = 1e-6 and atol = 1e-4, satisfy it, and a certificate computed with a coarser rtol needs a larger atol.

Stability

The rate, smallest or given

c.rate is an upper bound on the joint spectral radius. Given a rate, certify solves at that rate only, and is_feasible says whether it is certified:

at(rate) = PCC.certify(template, graph, PCC.StabilityProblem(system);
                       optimizer = Clarabel.Optimizer, rate)
lyapunov.rate, PCC.is_feasible(at(0.95)), PCC.is_feasible(at(1.0))
(0.9586944580078125, false, true)

A lower bound, and exactness

jsr_lower_bound gives a lower bound on the joint spectral radius, from the cycles of tight edges. is_jsr_exact tests a condition under which the rate equals the joint spectral radius; false means unknown. optimality_gap is 0 when that condition holds, and the rate minus the lower bound otherwise:

PCC.jsr_lower_bound(lyapunov), PCC.is_jsr_exact(lyapunov), PCC.optimality_gap(lyapunov)
(0.8001574042773703, false, 0.15853705373044225)

The joint spectral radius of the running example therefore lies between the lower bound and the rate. Another template or another graph may lower the rate: see Choosing a template and Choosing a graph.

The dual certificate

dual(c) gives a certificate on the dual graph, for the transposed system, at the same rate, without solving anything: see Duality.

A complete example: Bound the joint spectral radius of my system.

Safety

c.margin is the margin by which the barrier separates the initial set from the unsafe set, positive when safety is certified:

barrier.margin
0.28238630378039326

c.initial_multipliers and c.unsafe_multipliers hold the multipliers of the S-procedure for the two sets, one per node.

A complete example: Prove my system safe.

Optimal control

c.gains holds one gain per node:

controller.gains
1-element Vector{Matrix{Float64}}:
 [-0.14285646166407995 -0.5160743432311196]

simulate(c, x0, modes) runs the policy of the certificate, which applies at each state the gain of the node whose function is smallest there. The inputs it applies along four modes:

trajectory = PCC.simulate(controller, [1.0, 1.0], [1, 2, 2, 1])
PCC.inputs(trajectory)
4-element Vector{Vector{Float64}}:
 [-0.6589308048951995]
 [-0.7143676383430481]
 [-0.06245586711833694]
 [-0.05084435800039741]
