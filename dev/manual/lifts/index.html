Lifts

A lift is an operation on a path-complete graph, and refine is an algorithm that applies lifts. A lift L maps a graph $G$ to a path-complete graph $L(G)$ that is no worse than $G$ for every template closed under the operations it requires: a certificate on $G$ gives one on $L(G)$. The literature calls every operation below a lift.

Lifts serve two uses. refine applies them, one step at a time, where a certificate is tight; see Graph refinement. compare asks whether $L(G)$ simulates another graph $H$, which proves $G \le_{\mathcal V} H$; see Graph comparison via lifts.

Applying a lift

lift(L, G) applies L to the whole graph, lift(L, G, edges) at each of the given edges, and lift(L, G, node) at a node; a lift has the forms its definition gives it, and another form throws a MethodError. A lift applied at edges is applied at every edge when none are given. The result is a Lifted: the graph and the origins of its nodes, what each stands for in $G$.

Validity

A lift is valid for a template on a system when $G \le_{\mathcal V} L(G)$ for every path-complete $G$ ((Debauche et al., 2022), Defs. 6 and 7). is_valid reads it from requirements: the template, or its dual on the dual system, must be closed under each operation listed, as returned by is_closed_under and tabulated on the Templates page. A lift that requires nothing is valid for every template: the splits give each copy the function of the node it came from ((Ninite and Jungers, 2026), Thm. 3), and the products chain two edge inequalities ((Athanasopoulos and Jungers, 2019), Thm. 1).

Splits and products

The splits and products act at an edge $(s, d, w)$, forward or backward.

Drawn at the edge s ─w─▶ d of a graph where s also reads u to f and is entered by a, and d is also entered by b and leaves for e:

      before                      OutSplitLift               InSplitLift

 a ──v──▶ s ──w──▶ d ──x──▶ e     a ──v──▶ s′ ──w──▶ d ──x──▶ e   a ──v──▶ s ──w──▶ d′ ──x──▶ e
          │        ▲              │                  ▲                     │
          u        t              v                  t                     u    b ──t──▶ d ──x──▶ e
          ▼        │              ▼                  │                     ▼
          f        b              s ──u──▶ f         b                     f

      ForwardProductLift                              BackwardProductLift

 a ──v──▶ s ──w·x──▶ e                                a ──v·w──▶ d ──x──▶ e
          │                                           │          ▲
          u          b ──t──▶ d ──x──▶ e              v          t
          ▼                                           ▼          │
          f                                           s ──u──▶ f b

A split adds a node and splits the inequalities that passed through it. A product adds no node and writes a word on an edge: one inequality on $A_x A_w$ with nothing imposed on the state in between, which is sound for stability only. A WordGraph carries words, and its expanded_form reads the same language with one mode per edge; for a template closed under composition the two certify the same rate, the word graph with fewer variables ((Debauche, 2024), Prop. 7.70).

At a node, OutSplitLift makes one copy per outgoing edge, owning that edge, as in (Ninite and Jungers, 2026), Def. 3; at an edge it makes one copy owning that edge, so the lift at a node is the lift at all its outgoing edges. On the one-node graph it gives the dual De Bruijn graph of order 1.

Each backward lift is the dual of the forward one, dual(L)(G) = dual(L(dual(G))), and the classical hierarchies are lifts of the whole graph:

  • InSplitLift at every edge is PathDependentLift(1); iterated from the one-node graph it gives the de_bruijn graphs;
  • either product at every edge is ProductLift(2).
PathCompleteCertificates.lift — Function
lift(L, graph) -> Lifted
lift(L, graph, edges) -> Lifted
lift(L, graph, node) -> Lifted

Apply L to the whole graph, at each of the given edges, or at a node. A lift applied at edges is applied at every edge when none are given; the splits are also applied at a node. A lift without the form asked for throws a MethodError.
