Lifts

A lift turns a path-complete graph into another path-complete graph. It is valid for a template when every certificate on the graph, in that template, gives one on the lifted graph with the same value ((Debauche et al., 2022), Def. 6): for that template, the lifted graph is then at least as good. Lifts are the steps of refinement, which applies them where a certificate is tight, and the tools of comparison, which uses them to prove that a graph is at least as good as another.

Why a lift never makes the certificate worse

A lift builds the new graph from the old one, and a certificate on the new graph from a certificate on the old one, in one of four ways:

  • Copy. A split replaces a node by copies, and each copy takes the function of the node: every edge inequality of the new graph is one of the old ones.
  • Chain. A product replaces an edge by its products with the edges that follow or precede it: two chained edge inequalities imply the inequality of their product ((Athanasopoulos and Jungers, 2019)).
  • Combine. The min, max and sum lifts make a node from a set of nodes, whose function is the minimum, the maximum or the sum of theirs.
  • Compose. The composition lifts compose node functions with the dynamics.

Copying and chaining need nothing from the template, so they are valid for every template. Combining and composing need a template closed under the operation ((Debauche et al., 2022), Def. 7): the sum of two quadratic forms is a quadratic form, so the sum lift is valid for the quadratic template, but their minimum is not, so the min lift is not. A lift is also valid when the dual template is closed under the dual operation: on positive systems, the min lift is valid for the linear copositive template, whose dual is closed under maximum ((Debauche, 2024), Thm. 7.43). is_valid(L, template, system) says whether a lift is valid, and What works with what lists the lifts valid for each template.

Every lift also keeps the graph path-complete, so a lifted graph needs no new check.

Applying a lift

lift(L, graph) applies the lift L to the whole graph, lift(L, graph, edges) at the given edges, and lift(L, graph, node) at one node; each lift has the forms its definition allows. The result is a Lifted: the new graph, graph, and the origins of its nodes, what each stands for in the old graph.

Splitting the node of the one-node graph, one copy per outgoing edge, gives the dual De Bruijn graph of order 1, drawn in Choosing a graph:

import PathCompleteCertificates as PCC
lifted = PCC.lift(PCC.OutSplitLift(), PCC.common_graph(2), 1)
PCC.n_nodes(PCC.graph(lifted)), PCC.origins(lifted)
(2, [1, 1])

Both new nodes are copies of node 1.

The lifts

The lifts fall into the four groups above, plus the identity. Within a group, they come in dual pairs: the out- and in-splits, the forward and backward products, the min and max lifts, the forward and backward compositions (see Duality). Refinement applies splits and products at nodes or edges; comparison uses the identity, the combinations and the compositions.

Splits: copying nodes

Valid for every template.

LiftWhat it doesUsed by
OutSplitLiftcopies a node, one copy per outgoing edge, at a node or at given edgesboth refinement algorithms
InSplitLiftcopies a node, one copy per incoming edge, at a node or at given edgesBestOfTightEdgeLifts
PathDependentLift(M)in-splits every edge, $M$ times: from the one-node graph, the De Bruijn graph of order $M$none

Products: chaining edges

Valid for every template. A product writes a sequence of modes on an edge, which stability alone supports.

BackwardProductLiftreplaces the given edges by their products with the edges that precede themBestOfTightEdgeLifts
ProductLift(T)replaces every edge by the paths of $T$ edgesnone

Compositions

Valid for the templates closed under composition with the matrices of the system, for the forward lift, or with their inverses, for the backward one.

The identity

IdentityLift returns the graph unchanged: with it, compare tests a plain simulation, which holds for every template.

Adding a lift

A new lift is one file of code, in src/lifts/catalogue/: see Extending the package.
