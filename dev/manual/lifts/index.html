Lifts

A lift maps a graph to another and keeps it path-complete, so a lifted graph needs no new check (Debauche et al., 2022; Def. 5). It is valid for a template when the lifted graph is at least as good as the original for that template (see Comparing graphs) (Debauche et al., 2022; Def. 6). Applying a lift is an operation on the graph only, which solves nothing.

Lifts and their validity are established for stability. They are the steps of refinement, which applies them where a certificate is tight, and the tools of comparison, which uses them to prove that a graph is at least as good as another. They fall into two categories, below, and come in dual pairs (see Duality).

Applying a lift

lift(L, graph) applies the lift L to the whole graph, lift(L, graph, edges) at the given edges, and lift(L, graph, node) at one node. The splits take all three forms, the forward and backward products the first two, and the other lifts the whole graph only. The result is a Lifted: the new graph, get_graph, and the origins of its nodes, what each stands for in the old graph.

Splitting the node of the common graph, one copy per outgoing edge, gives the dual De Bruijn graph of order 1, drawn in Choosing a graph:

using PathCompleteCertificates
lifted = lift(OutSplitLift(), common_graph(2), 1)
n_nodes(get_graph(lifted)), origins(lifted)
(2, [1, 1])

Both new nodes are copies of node 1.

Splits and products

Four local operations at an edge $(s, d, i)$ are valid for every template (Athanasopoulos and Jungers, 2019; Defs. 4 to 7 and Thm. 1):

  • the forward product replaces $(s, d, i)$ by its products with the edges leaving $d$, which bypasses $d$.
  • the backward product replaces $(s, d, i)$ by its products with the edges entering $s$, which bypasses $s$.
  • the in-split adds a copy $d'$ of $d$, with the same outgoing edges, and replaces $(s, d, i)$ by $(s, d', i)$.
  • the out-split adds a copy $s'$ of $s$, with the same incoming edges, and replaces $(s, d, i)$ by $(s', d, i)$.

A split copies a node function: each copy takes the function of its node, so every edge inequality of the new graph is one of the old ones. A product chains two edge inequalities, which imply the inequality of their product. So neither needs anything from the template. A split adds a node, and a product adds none but writes a sequence of modes on an edge, which only stability supports (see What works with what).

Combinations and compositions

The min, max and sum lifts make a node from a set of nodes, a multiset for the sum lift, whose function is the minimum, the maximum or the sum of theirs (Debauche et al., 2022; Defs. 8 and 9). The composition lifts compose the node functions with the dynamics (Debauche, 2024; Def. 7.58), extended to compositions of any length by Jongeneel and Jungers (2025).

Each is valid for the templates closed under its operation (Debauche et al., 2022; Def. 7 and Thm. 3). The sum of two quadratic forms is a quadratic form, so the sum lift is valid for the quadratic template, but their minimum is not, so the min lift is not. A lift is also valid when its dual lift is valid for the dual template (Debauche, 2024; Prop. 7.5): on positive systems, the min lift is valid for the linear copositive template, whose dual is closed under maximum (Debauche, 2024; Thm. 7.43). is_valid(L, template, system) says whether a lift is valid, and What works with what lists the lifts valid for each template.

These lifts serve to compare graphs (see Comparing graphs).

The identity

IdentityLift returns the graph unchanged: with it, compare tests a plain simulation, which holds for every template.
