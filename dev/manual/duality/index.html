Duality

Graphs, systems, lifts, and some templates and certificates have a dual: reverse the edges of a graph, transpose the matrices of a system, replace each node function by its dual. dual computes it, and applying it twice gives the object back. For stability, duality turns a certificate into another one, on the reversed graph, for the transposed system, at the same rate. It is a tool on the framework itself: each graph, lift and certificate gets a counterpart at no cost.

Why it holds: for a template of norms, node functions form a certificate on a graph for a system exactly when their dual norms form one on the dual graph for the transposed system ((Debauche, 2024), Lemma 6.25). The numbers below refer to the same thesis.

The code on this page uses the running example of Systems and problems: system, and problem = PCC.StabilityProblem(system), solved with Clarabel.

The dual of each object

dual ofgives
a graphthe same nodes with every edge reversed, path-complete exactly when the graph is (Prop. 6.6)
a systemthe same modes with every matrix transposed
a stability problemthe stability problem of the dual system
a templatethe template of the dual functions, when has_dual holds
a stability certificatethe dual functions on the dual graph, for the dual system, at the same rate
a lift $L$the lift that applies $L$ to the dual graph and reverses the result: $\operatorname{dual}(L)(G) = \operatorname{dual}(L(\operatorname{dual}(G)))$

Quadratic forms are their own duals, and the linear copositive and dual copositive templates are each other's. The other templates have no dual here.

What you gain

A second certificate, without solving. On the running example:

c = PCC.certify(PCC.QuadraticTemplate(), PCC.common_graph(system), problem; optimizer = Clarabel.Optimizer)
d = PCC.dual(c)
c.rate == d.rate
true

A second hierarchy of graphs. The dual De Bruijn graphs, dual(de_bruijn(k, M)), read the next modes where the primal ones read the last; see Choosing a graph. Refinement meets them: its first step from the one-node graph gives the dual De Bruijn graph of order 1.

The backward lifts. InSplitLift, BackwardProductLift, MaxLift and BackwardCompositionLift are the duals of OutSplitLift, ForwardProductLift, MinLift and CompositionLift.

Comparisons through the dual. $G \le_{\mathcal V} H$ exactly when $\operatorname{dual}(G) \le_{\mathcal V^*} \operatorname{dual}(H)$, with $\mathcal V^*$ the template of the dual norms (Prop. 6.27), and a lift is valid for a template exactly when its dual lift is valid for the dual template (Prop. 7.5). So, on positive systems, the min lift is valid for the linear copositive template because the dual copositive template is closed under maximum, and the max lift, the dual of the min lift (Lemma 7.45), is decided by the relation of the min lift on the dual graphs. See Lifts and Comparing graphs.
