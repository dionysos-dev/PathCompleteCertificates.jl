Graph comparison via lifts

Given two path-complete graphs $G$ and $H$, we say that $H$ is at least as good as $G$ for a template $\mathcal V$, written $G \le_{\mathcal V} H$, if every switched system admitting a certificate on $G$ with functions in $\mathcal V$ also admits one on $H$ with the same template. In particular, any bound on the joint spectral radius obtained with $G$ can also be obtained with $H$ ((Debauche et al., 2022), Def. 4).

This order depends on the template. When the template is closed under an operation, the order is characterised by a simulation of $H$ by a transformation of $G$, the lift of that operation. compare tests every lift, and the closures of a template determine which of the results hold for it. Comparing graphs: five graphs, three templates does this on the five graphs of (Debauche et al., 2023).

The theory is stated for arbitrary switching. A simulation found by compare depends on the graphs only, so it transfers a certificate on $G$ to one on $H$ for every system for which both graphs are path-complete. The converse, that the graphs are not ordered when there is no simulation, is proved for arbitrary switching only.

The template-free order

$G$ simulates $H$ when there is a map $R$ from the nodes of $H$ to those of $G$ with $(R(a), R(b), i)$ an edge of $G$ for every edge $(a, b, i)$ of $H$ ((Philippe and Jungers, 2019), Def. 3.1). Read a certificate on $G$ through $R$ and it is one on $H$, in any template. The converse holds too: $G \le_{\mathcal V} H$ for every template exactly when $G$ simulates $H$ (Thm. 3.5). simulation(G, H) returns the map, or nothing.

When no map exists, the graphs may still be ordered for a particular template; the lifts below decide this.

The lifts that decide the order

A lift that combines node functions under an operation encodes the inequalities those combinations inherit ((Debauche et al., 2022), Defs. 5 to 9). When the lifted $G$ simulates $H$, $G \le_{\mathcal V} H$ for every template closed under the operation; for the min, max and sum lifts the converse holds as well. simulation(lift, G, H) decides it, by the method in the third column. The splits add nothing here: their origins are a simulation of the lifted graph by $G$, so whenever the lifted graph simulates $H$, $G$ does too and the identity lift finds the order. The products give graphs with words on their edges, which compare does not read.

compare

c = compare(G, H; templates = (QuadraticTemplate(),), system, optimizer)
lifts(c)        # the lifts tried, in order
simulations(c)  # lift => simulation, for each lift whose lifted G simulates H
templates(c)    # the templates given for which H is proved no worse than G

The lifts are tried in this order: the identity lift, whose map settles the question for every template and ends the search; the min and max lifts, by a relation; the sum lift, by the linear program, only when an optimizer is given; the two composition lifts of length one. Other lifts are passed with lifts. A template is listed in templates(c) when some lift with a simulation is valid for it on system, which is required with templates.

Without templates, a Comparison states that $H$ is no worse than $G$ for every template closed under the requirements of each lift in simulations.

Graphs no better than a common Lyapunov function

The one-node graph, common_graph of the system, simulates every graph, so $G_0 \le_{\mathcal V} G$ always. The other direction, compare(G, common_graph(system)), asks whether every certificate on $G$ yields a common Lyapunov function with the same rate. For a min-closed template it holds exactly when the min lift simulates the one-node graph ((Debauche, 2024), Thm. 8.1): when some set of nodes of $G$ has, from each of its nodes and for each mode, an edge reading that mode back into the set, so that the minimum of their functions is a common function. A complete graph always has one, the whole graph ((Debauche et al., 2022), Prop. 3; (Debauche, 2024), Cor. 7.53). The relation of the min lift finds the largest such set, and the max lift asks the same of the reversed edges for max-closed templates. Comparing graphs: five graphs, three templates shows both answers on the five graphs.

Under duality

$G \le_{\mathcal V} H$ holds exactly when $\mathrm{dual}(G) \le_{\mathcal V^*} \mathrm{dual}(H)$ ((Debauche, 2024), Prop. 6.27). Every backward lift is therefore the dual of a forward one and decides its order on the dual graphs, and a question about the dual copositive template is the same question about the linear copositive template on the transposed system. See Duality.
