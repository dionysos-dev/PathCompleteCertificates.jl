What works with what

This page says which combinations the package supports: the templates each problem accepts, the lifts valid for each template, and where refinement and comparison apply. It is also a map of what is missing. The package aims to implement the state of the art on path-complete certificates, and each gap below is a place where research or a contribution can close it.

Templates and problems

For each template, the program that each problem solves with it, linear (LP), second-order cone (SOCP) or semidefinite (SDP):

TemplateStabilityProblemSafetyProblemOptimalControlProblem
QuadraticTemplate()SDPSDPSDP
PiecewiseQuadraticTemplate(P) †SDPSDPSDP §
SumOfSquaresTemplate(r) †SDPSDPSDP §
PiecewiseLinearTemplate(P)LPSOCP ‡LP or SOCP §
DualPiecewiseLinearTemplate(P)LPSOCP ‡×
LinearCopositiveTemplate() *LP××
DualCopositiveTemplate() *LP××

* For positive systems, whose matrices have nonnegative entries.

† Not available to refine: its edge slacks are not implemented.

‡ For an ellipsoidal initial set $X_0$ and an unsafe set $X_u$ outside an ellipsoid.

§ With fixed gains, by default those of the quadratic certificate. Polynomial node functions must be convex, with SumOfSquaresTemplate(r; convex = true). The piecewise linear template solves a linear program for p = 1 and a second-order cone program for p = 2, whether Q and R are matrices or polytopes.

× Not supported: see The gaps.

For optimal control, the exponent p of the cost (see Systems and problems) must match the template: 2 for the quadratic and piecewise quadratic templates, $2r$ for SumOfSquaresTemplate(r; convex = true), and 1 or 2 for the piecewise linear template, whose node functions are then the gauges $g_{\Omega_s}^p$. A cost given by polytopes $\mathcal Q$ and $\mathcal R$ works with the piecewise linear template only. The other templates need the matrices $Q$ and $R$.

Unsupported combinations report an error before anything is solved. Two more conditions are on the graph:

  • Optimal control needs a complete graph, not only a path-complete one.
  • A WordGraph, with sequences of modes on its edges, is accepted by stability only. For safety and optimal control, the states inside a word would go unchecked.

Lifts and templates

The identity and the lifts used by refinement, the splits and the products, are valid for every template. For the lifts that combine the node functions or compose them with the dynamics:

* For positive systems, as in the table above.

¹ If the matrices are invertible.

² If every node has the same partition.

³ If every node has the same partition, and it is a cross-polytope.

⁴ If no matrix has a zero column.

⁵ If the matrices are invertible with nonnegative inverses.

⁶ If no matrix has a zero row.

× Not valid: the template is not closed under the operation of the lift, nor its dual under the dual operation.

Some of these lifts are valid through the dual template, which is closed under the dual operation (see Combinations and compositions). is_valid(L, template, system) answers for a given template and system.

Refinement and comparison

refine runs on StabilityProblem only, with the templates without † in the table of templates and problems. compare reads graphs with one mode per edge.

Duals

dual applies to graphs, lifts, systems without input, stability problems and stability certificates. Among the templates, the quadratic one is its own dual, and the piecewise linear and copositive pairs are each other's. The sum-of-squares and piecewise quadratic templates have no dual, and neither do safety and optimal control.

The gaps

Each gap is either a method the package does not implement yet, or a question the literature leaves open. Extending the package says where a new template, problem, lift or refinement strategy goes.

Not implemented yet:

  • The × cells of the table: safety and optimal control with the copositive templates, and optimal control with the dual piecewise linear template.
  • Polyhedral safety with other sets, such as a bounded unsafe obstacle.
  • Optimal control on graphs that are not complete: the convex program covers complete graphs only.
  • Refinement with sums of squares and piecewise quadratic functions: their edge slacks need an optimization of their own.
  • Constrained switching, for instance by an automaton: planned, see issue #45.
  • Further problems of the path-complete literature, such as stabilization.

Open questions:

  • Joint optimal-control synthesis beyond the quadratic template: the other templates use fixed gains. Computing the gains and the node functions together needs a convex reformulation or a nonconvex algorithm.
  • Refinement for safety and optimal control: the results that guide refinement, where to lift and when to stop, are proved for stability.
